What it is

It works and looks like the Stage Manager built into macOS, plus what people have asked Apple for since 2022. None of it changes how Apple’s clicks, drags and keys work; if you never use it, it behaves exactly like Apple’s.

Stages after a restart

Your stages come back after a restart, in the same order.

Combine stages

Drop one card on another to combine the two stages.

Your own card order

Keep the cards in your own order, or in your Dock’s order (Settings).

Open an app here

Hold Shift while opening an app to put it in the stage you are in, not a new one.

Keyboard shortcuts

Switch stages, or send a window to one, with ⌃⌥1–9 (turned on in Settings).

Windows clear of the strip

Windows move just clear of the strip, so the cards stay in view (Settings).

More in the config file

Everything else is in one plain text file. An AI assistant can edit it for you,
and Settings → Advanced shows how.

  • Rules for new windowsBy app and title: a named stage, every stage, centred or cascaded.
  • Named stagesNames and numbers on the cards.
  • The stripWidth, size, tilt, an edge tab, above or below windows.
  • MotionEvery animation’s timing, measured from Apple’s.
  • More shortcutsNew stage, pin a window, show or hide the strip.

First launch

A small welcome window walks you through it.

  • Turn off Apple’s Stage ManagerIt offers to do it for you.
  • Accessibility RequiredTo move your windows.
  • Screen Recording OptionalFor live pictures of your windows on the cards.

Everything about your windows stays on your Mac. Stage Manager only goes online for a daily update check on GitHub, and you can turn that off.
