What it is

It works and looks like the Stage Manager built into macOS: recent stages wait as cards on the left, and the windows you are using stay in the middle, where you put them. If you know Apple’s, you already know this one.

It also adds what people have asked Apple for since 2022. None of it changes how Apple’s clicks, drags and keys work; if you never use it, it behaves exactly like Apple’s.

  • Your stages come back after a restart, in the same order.
  • Hold Shift while opening an app to put it in the stage you are in, not a new one.
  • Drop one card on another to combine the two stages.
  • Switch stages, or send a window to one, from the keyboard (⌃⌥1–9, turned on in Settings).
  • Keep the cards in your own order, or in your Dock’s order (Settings).
  • Name your stages, and set where new windows go (config file).

The config file is one plain text file. An AI assistant can edit it for you, and Settings → Advanced shows how.

First launch

A small welcome window walks you through it.

  • Turn off Apple’s Stage ManagerIt offers to do it for you.
  • Accessibility RequiredTo move your windows.
  • Screen Recording OptionalFor live pictures of your windows on the cards.

Everything about your windows stays on your Mac. Stage Manager only goes online for a daily update check on GitHub, and you can turn that off.
